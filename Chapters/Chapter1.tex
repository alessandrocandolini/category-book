\chapter{Categories}
\label{chap:chap1}

The set-theoretical approach to algebraic structures involves the study of sets and functions defined on those sets and satisfying given properties. In other words, sets and functions provide the basic ingredients to bake those constructions. The starting point of category theory can be taken to be the observation that sets and functions happen to be just too restrictive as a ``vocabulary'', and a higher level of abstraction can be reached if we embrace the viewpoint of working with more general building blocks: objects and morphisms. This chapter is dedicated to an exploration of the general definition of a category, its basic properties, and how several traditional algebraic structures fits into the categorical picture.
